International market-entry checklist for B2B software teams

Before a new-country launch becomes a roadmap, validate what buyers need, how you will reach them, and what the business must support. This checklist turns those conversations into a practical go/no-go review.

Short answer: A strong market-entry plan does not begin with a country shortlist. It begins with evidence that a defined customer can buy, use, and be supported by your product in that market.

The six checks to complete before you launch

  1. Define one customer and one job to be done

    Name the buyer, the user, the trigger event, and the problem your product solves. A country is not a segment; avoid evaluating a market before you can describe who will choose the product and why.

  2. Test demand with observable evidence

    Review qualified conversations, pipeline signals, procurement patterns, relevant search demand, and the alternatives buyers already use. Record what would disprove the opportunity as well as what supports it.

  3. Choose a route to market

    Decide whether the first motion is direct sales, a local partner, a marketplace, a channel relationship, or a product-led route. Each choice changes the required messaging, commercial terms, and operating effort.

  4. Map compliance questions before promising a date

    Identify the questions that require local answers: company structure, contracts, tax, employment, payments, data protection, sector rules, and procurement. This is a discovery list—not legal advice.

  5. Plan the local product experience

    List the language, currency, billing, support hours, integrations, accessibility, and trust signals that buyers will expect. Distinguish launch-critical work from improvements that can follow real demand.

  6. Set a reversible launch experiment

    Define the smallest launch that can answer your central question. Assign an owner, budget boundary, decision date, success measure, and a clear condition for pausing or expanding the effort.

What a decision-ready market brief should contain

Buyer hypothesis

The ideal customer, buying committee, priority use case, and evidence from discovery—not a generic industry label.

Market assumptions

The assumptions about demand, competition, pricing, regulation, and partner access that still need to be tested.

Launch scope

The first offer, first channel, product changes, and operational responsibilities required for a limited launch.

Decision rule

The measurable signal that tells the team to continue, adjust, pause, or choose a different market.

Frequently asked questions

When should we use this checklist?

Use it before approving a launch budget, local hire, partnership, or country-specific product project. It makes the most important assumptions visible while they are still cheap to test.

What should we validate first?

Start with buyer demand and your route to market. A detailed launch plan has limited value until you know who can buy, why they would switch, and how you will reach them.

Is this a substitute for local legal or tax advice?

No. Use it to surface the questions early, then engage qualified local advisers for country-specific legal, tax, employment, and data-protection guidance.
