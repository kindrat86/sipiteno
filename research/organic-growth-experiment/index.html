The organic growth experiment

We published far too many search pages, learned that ranking for weak queries is not growth, and started removing the machinery that created the problem. This is Sipiteno's public account of the baseline, the decisions, and the results.

Published 15 September 2026. Updated when the underlying measurements change.

The uncomfortable finding: indexing was not the constraint. A page can rank and still be useless if it answers a query that no buyer asks. Our new operating metric is the number of pages that earn a click, not the number of pages that exist.

Where the experiment started

On 11 August 2026, we measured the search performance of the portfolio that includes Sipiteno. The scale looked impressive until we compared it with actual traffic.

Baseline measureObserved value
Published URLs16,691
Organic clicks in the trailing 28 days134
Click-through rate0.27%
URLs ranking in the top 102,921
Ranked URLs that earned a click98
Published URLs that earned no click99.4%

The contradiction changed the strategy. Thousands of pages had achieved the technical outcome they were built for, but almost none had earned attention. Adding more pages would have multiplied the failure mode rather than solving it.

What we stopped doing

Page count as progress

A generated URL is now a liability until it demonstrates demand or carries unique data. Shipping volume is not reported as a win.

Rank without demand

Positions and impressions are diagnostic signals. They do not matter when the underlying query has no useful audience.

Unsupported authority

Fabricated bylines, office presence, and unsourced performance claims are being removed. Trust is harder to rebuild than a page.

What changed on Sipiteno

We removed hidden prerendered text, deleted a fabricated research byline, replaced false local-presence wording with remote-delivery language, consolidated duplicate country-service routes, and stopped advertising pages that did not exist. The work is deliberately subtractive.

The next phase is to publish fewer things that are independently useful: source manifests, downloadable evidence, decision tools, and honest postmortems. Every new indexable page must replace an existing weak page. This log replaces the old Dream list page.

What we expect to learn

We are testing whether a smaller, more honest site can earn attention from a wider set of pages than a large templated site. Falling impressions can be healthy when low-value queries disappear. The signals that matter are clicked URLs, qualified demand, references from independent sites, and whether a useful artifact is downloaded or cited.

If those signals do not improve, the experiment should say so plainly. The point is not to make Sipiteno look right in retrospect. The point is to create evidence strong enough to change what we do next.
